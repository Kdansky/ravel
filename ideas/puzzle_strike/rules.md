# Puzzle Strike: Bag of Chips — Rules

This is **Puzzle Strike: Bag of Chips**, Sirlin Games, Third Edition
(rules v7.3, 2013) — the stable form of the original game. **Not**
"Puzzle Strike 2," a 2022 Kickstarter sequel the designer himself calls
*"really just two different games"* sharing only the name, the ≥10-loses
loss condition and the ten-character framing — see §11. Where this
document says "Puzzle Strike" with no qualifier it means the original,
Third Edition.

**Sources.** The primary source is the official rulebook PDF itself,
fetched and read in full (16 pages): [Puzzle Strike Rulebook v7.3](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/50f14d35e4b0d70ab5fc4f24/t/53f858c5e4b01724194441bb/1408784581942/puzzlestrike_rulebook_7-3_web.pdf),
linked from [Sirlin Games' rulebooks index](https://www.sirlingames.com/rulebooks).
Every unattributed quote below is verbatim from it. Secondary sources:
David Sirlin's own design article ["Designing Puzzle Strike"](https://www.sirlin.net/articles/designing-puzzle-strike)
(sirlin.net), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puzzle_Strike:_Bag_of_Chips),
a [Club Fantasci review](https://clubfantasci.wordpress.com/2013/07/26/puzzle-strike-bag-of-chips-3rd-ed-review-by-david-lowry/)
of the 3rd Edition, and three character strategy-guide threads on

**One correction to flag up front, since the objective that spawned this
document assumed otherwise.** Puzzle Strike's wound/damage chip is
called **Wound**, not "Crashing Moles" — that name was searched for
specifically across the rulebook, sirlin.net and BGG and found nowhere.
Nor does the shared bank use gemstone flavor names (Ruby, Emerald, …):
gem chips are named purely by number — **1-gem, 2-gem, 3-gem, 4-gem**.
Both corrections matter for §5–6 below.

## 1. Overview

2–4 players, ~20 minutes **[the rulebook gives no minutes figure
directly; ~20 min is cross-confirmed by two independent secondary
sources — Wikipedia and the Club Fantasci review — so treated as
reasonably solid despite not being in the primary source]**. The
rulebook singles out one configuration as the competitive default:
*"The 2-player game mode is usually used in tournaments."* (Tournament
Rules, p.11) Free-for-all (3–4p) and a 2v2 Team Battle mode exist and
are fully rostered, but 2-player is both the design center and the
mode this document treats as primary.

The game's own back-of-box framing (p.3):

> "Puzzle Strike is a deck building game that simulates a puzzle game
> played amongst Fantasy Strike characters. In this puzzle game, gems
> fall into each player's gem pile every turn, and whoever's gem pile
> fills up first loses. You combine gems in your own gem pile to create
> bigger gems, then crash those gems to break them apart and send them
> to other players. The more full your gem pile is, the closer you are
> to losing, but the more chips you get to draw each turn to make a
> comeback."

**The load-bearing structural fact, easy to miss if modeling this off
Dominion:** every player has *two* independent personal chip pools, not
one. A **deck** (bag/hand/discard, cycling exactly like a Dominion
deck — the buy-and-build economy, §4) and a separate **gem pile** (never
shuffled, never drawn from, holds only Gem chips, and its running total
is the entire loss condition, §6). Gem chips that reach your gem pile
and gem chips that pass through your deck as spendable money are, for
most of a game, different physical chips serving different jobs, even
though both are printed "1-gem" etc.

## 2. Turn structure

Every turn is four phases, always in this order — the rulebook's own
mnemonic: *"A, A, B, C are the four phases each turn: Ante, Action, Buy,
Cleanup."* (p.7)

1. **Ante.** *"At the beginning of your turn, take a 1-gem from the bank
   and put it in your gem pile."* Mandatory, not optional, and it goes
   straight to the **gem pile**, never the deck. Late-game "Panic Time"
   escalates this to bigger gems — see §9.
2. **Action.** Play up to one Action chip (any chip with a banner) from
   hand: put it face up on the table, resolve its text; it does not
   discard until Cleanup. Black arrows on a chip grant an extra action
   play; colored arrows (brown/red/blue/purple) grant one restricted to
   chips whose own banner matches that color.
3. **Buy.** *"You must buy at least one chip per turn."* Bought chips go
   straight to your **discard pile**, not your hand — *"buying now is
   planning for the future."* Money ("gem power") comes from playing
   Gem chips out of hand face up on the table (**not** gems in your gem
   pile — those never spend): *"Play as many Gem chips as you want from
   your hand to the table (not to your gem pile!), then add their
   values to any +gem power you might have gotten during this turn's
   action phase. Subtract $1 for each Combine you played this turn."*
   Unspent money is lost at Cleanup — it never carries over.
4. **Cleanup.** *"Make sure any chips you bought this turn are in your
   discard pile, put all the chips you played to the table this turn
   (including gem chips used as money) into your discard pile, and
   discard the chips in your hand. Then draw 5 chips."* Plus a **height
   bonus**, scaled to your current gem pile total: *"If your gem pile
   has 3 / 6 / 9 in it, then draw an extra +1 / +2 / +3 chips"* — on top
   of the base 5, so pile totals 3–5/6–8/9 draw 6/7/8 chips respectively.
   Being close to losing is also what lets you draw your way out of it.

**Turn order.** Not stated anywhere in the rulebook — no "youngest
player," "clockwise," or explicit first-player rule was found by
targeted search. The one indirect confirmation that an order exists at
all: free-for-all counter-crash priority passes *"in turn order,
starting with the player after you"* (p.10). **[unverified — starting
player and direction are left to table convention]**.

## 3. The buy phase and the shared bank

There are no flavor names — every gem chip is named by its number. Full
box contents (Third Edition, p.3 and the bank-setup diagram, p.6):

| Chip type | Count in box | Always in the bank? |
|---|---|---|
| Puzzle Chips | 120 (24 designs × 5 copies) | only 10 of the 24 designs, chosen per game |
| Character Chips | 30 (10 characters × 3 each) | only the ones in players' starting decks |
| Combine (purple) | 20 | yes |
| Crash Gem (purple) | 16 | yes |
| Double Crash Gem (purple) | 10 | yes |
| 1-gem | 64 | yes |
| 2-gem | 20 | yes |
| 3-gem | 16 | yes |
| 4-gem | 12 | yes |
| Wound | 24 | yes |
| Blank | 10 | — |
| **Total** | **342** | |

Setup rule: *"There are 24 different Puzzle chips, each with 5 copies.
Select just 10 of these 24 stacks, put them in the bank, and leave the
other 14 stacks of Puzzle chips in the box."* Everything else (gems,
purples, Wounds) is always fully present at its box quantity, minus
whatever starting decks already used. The rulebook notes this yields
*"411,863,760 different starting conditions"* in a 4-player game from
the Puzzle Chip selection alone.

**Money, precisely.** Not a persistent currency — it is computed fresh
each turn from Gem chips played out of hand as an action-phase-adjacent
spend (technically part of the Buy phase itself, per the phase order
above), plus bonus "+gem power" from played chips, minus $1 per Combine
played that turn. It never banks between turns. **Buying a Wound is the
mandatory fallback**, not a choice among equals: *"if you can't afford
to buy anything else, you'll have to buy a wound (they cost 0)... You
can and must buy a Wound if you have less than 0 money. You can't buy
more than one wound per turn. If there are no wounds left in the bank,
you don't have to buy a chip at all that turn."*

## 4. The personal chip cycle: bag, hand, discard — and two more zones

Structurally this **is** Dominion's draw/hand/discard/reshuffle loop,
confirmed near-verbatim in the rulebook's own words, with genuine
Puzzle-Strike-specific additions layered on top.

- **Draw pile = a physical bag**, randomized by shaking rather than
  riffle-shuffled: *"Shake your bag to shuffle, then draw 5 chips."*
  (Quick Start, p.3)
- **Hand size: 5**, both for the opening hand and every Cleanup draw
  (before the height bonus, §2) — identical to Dominion's baseline.
- **Discard pile.** Everything played to the table this turn (action
  chips, gems spent as money), the unplayed remainder of your hand, and
  every chip bought this turn, all land here at Cleanup. Bought chips
  are *not* accessible until they're later drawn — buying is planning
  ahead, exactly Dominion's shape.
- **Reshuffle trigger — matches Dominion exactly, mid-draw:** *"If an
  effect would make you draw a chip when your bag is empty, then put
  the chips from your discard pile (not including the chips you played
  to the table this turn) into your bag, shake your bag to shuffle
  them, then continue drawing."* (p.7) This is a genuine "reshuffle
  the *actual accumulated discard pile* into the draw pile mid-draw,"
  not a "recreate the starting contents" behavior — the distinction
  that matters for §7 of the Stage 2 gap table in
  [20-puzzle-strike.md](../20-puzzle-strike.md).
- **All discard piles are public**, browsable by any player at any
  time (More Rules, p.11) — again the Dominion norm.

**Two zones beyond hand/bag/discard, with no Dominion equivalent:**

- **The ongoing zone.** *"The ongoing zone is for chips that say
  'ongoing' on them: they stay in the ongoing zone until something says
  to discard them."* Playing an ongoing chip costs your one action for
  the turn; afterward it sits there granting a passive, no-further-cost
  benefit every subsequent turn until discarded by some other effect.
  Argagarg's Bubble Shield (§8) is one example.
- **The Piggy Bank option.** A chip-granted ability, not universal:
  *"During the cleanup phase, you may keep a chip in your hand that you
  didn't play rather than discard it. If you do, draw one less chip at
  the end of the turn."* Carries one unplayed hand chip forward at the
  cost of one fewer draw next turn.

**Starting deck is identical across all ten base characters** except
for exactly three character-specific chips: *"Take your 3 Character
chips, 1 Crash Gem chip, and 6 1-gem chips and put them in your bag.
This is your starting deck."* (p.5) Ten chips total, five drawn to the
opening hand. No base character was found with a different starting
deck *size* — every asymmetry lives in what those three named chips do.

## 5. Wounds

*"Wounds are useless chips that clog up your deck. Try to avoid them or
get rid of them if you can."* (p.5) Two facts correct the assumption
the objective's framing carried in:

**Wounds are mostly self-inflicted, not opponent-dealt.** The core
source is the "must buy at least one chip" rule (§3): if you can't or
won't afford anything else, you take a free (cost-0) Wound into your
own discard pile. This is an economic fallback baked into every
player's own Buy phase, not a universal "basic attack" any character
deals to another. Opponents *can* force Wounds onto you, but only via
specific character or puzzle-chip text — the clearest documented case
is Argagarg's **Hex of Murkwood** (§8), and it is Argagarg-specific, not
a base mechanic every character carries.

**Their effect while sitting in your pool is pure hand-clogging, not an
economic tax.** They carry no printed value the way Gem chips do (1–4)
and are not a gem denomination at all — a distinct chip type, cost 0,
worth nothing as money, doing nothing but occupying a hand slot and a
draw when you'd rather have drawn something useful. A community
strategy guide corroborates the framing directly: *"The longer the game
drags out, the more Argagarg's wounds and Protective Ward hurt his
opponent"* (Argagarg guide, forums.sirlingames.com — see §8).

**Wounds do not sit in the gem pile and are not counted by the loss
condition at all** — they live entirely in the deck cycle (hand/bag/
discard), which is a completely separate zone from the gem pile that
actually determines who loses (§6). Getting rid of them is handled by
scattered Puzzle-Chip/Character-Chip trash effects (e.g. Midori's Purge
Bad Habits implies a gain-from-bank-replacing-a-trashed-chip effect),
not a core rule — **[the exact universal-or-not trashing mechanism
wasn't independently confirmed; treated as chip-specific rather than a
base-game primitive]**.

## 6. The gem pile and the loss condition

A second, wholly separate personal zone. Nothing is ever drawn from it,
discarded from it in the ordinary sense, or shuffled — gems arrive via
Ante (§2) and via incoming Crashes (§7), and leave only via your own
Crash/Combine plays.

**Exact loss condition, verbatim (p.3, restated formally p.9):**

> "If you end your turn with the gems in your gem pile totaling 10 or
> more, you lose. (For example, four 1-gems, a 2-gem, and a 4-gem total
> 10.)"
>
> "Whenever a player ends his turn with his gem pile totaling 10 or
> more, he loses the game."

Precise mechanics worth getting exactly right:

- **It is the sum of printed gem *values*, not a count of chips.** Five
  physical chips (four 1-gems + one 2-gem + one 4-gem in the example
  above) is 10, the same as ten separate 1-gems would be.
- **Checked only at the end of your own turn, not continuously.** You
  can legally sit at 12+ mid-turn (an opponent just crashed a big gem at
  you) as long as you bring your own pile back under 10 — by crashing
  or combining your own gems — before your turn formally ends. Sirlin's
  design note states this explicitly: *"If you end your turn with a
  pile height of 10 or more, you lose, but you CAN have more than that
  during your turn."*
- **2-player:** the instant either player crosses the threshold at
  their own turn's end, the game is over — they lose, the other wins.
- **3–4 player free-for-all: the whole game ends the moment *any*
  player crosses 10 at their turn's end**, and the winner is *not*
  "whoever is left" — it is *"the player with the lowest gem pile total
  [that] wins the game. Yes, really!"* (p.9) — an instant, single-shot
  ending compared across every player's current total, not a
  last-one-standing elimination. Ties for lowest trigger sudden-death
  extra turns among just the tied players.
- **2v2 Team Battle:** teammates share one normal-sized gem pile (while
  keeping separate hands/bags/discards/actions/buys); the team loses
  together the instant that shared pile crosses 10.
- **An official "Last Man Standing" variant** (elimination rather than
  instant-end) exists but the rulebook actively steers away from it:
  *"This mode isn't recommended because it suffers from the same
  problems of almost all free-for-all modes: teaming up to knock
  someone else out is too powerful."* (p.10)
- **Floating gems (FFA only).** If your pile is already ≥10 *during
  someone else's turn* (before the game has actually ended, since the
  check only fires at turn-end), gems that would be sent to you
  "float" rather than landing, resolving the moment your pile next
  drops to 9 or less; if you end your own turn still ≥10 with gems
  still floating, those floating gems are simply destroyed rather than
  delivered. (p.10) **[a genuine multiplayer-only wrinkle; not relevant
  to the 2-player game, which has nobody left to float gems from]**

## 7. The Crash mechanic (and Combine)

**This does not match "N chips of the same printed value together in
one zone auto-crash."** That guess turns out to describe the *unrelated
2022 sequel's* mechanic (see §11), not the base/Third-Edition game. In
the actual game, a Crash is a **deliberate action-chip play**, always
targeting gems in **your own** gem pile, never an opponent's, and never
triggered automatically by matching counts:

> "Crashes always break gems in your own gem pile, never any one
> else's." (p.10)

### Combine (purple, 20 in box)

> "Combines two gems in your gem pile into a single gem if the total is
> 4 or less. For example you can combine a 1-gem and 2-gem into a
> 3-gem. (Put the 1-gem and 2-gem in the bank, then put a 3-gem from
> the bank into your gem pile.) The -$1 means you have less gem power
> (money) to spend during the buy phase. The arrow on the Combine means
> you get to play another action afterwards."

Legal even when it merges nothing (purely to chain the bonus arrow).
Valid combines in the base game are a small closed set given the ≤4
cap and only four denominations: 1+1→2, 1+2→3, 1+3→4, 2+2→4.

### Crash Gem (purple, 16 in box)

> "Crash Gems break gems in your gem pile and send them to any opponent
> you want. If you crash a 1-gem from your gem pile, you get rid of it
> and send it to an opponent's gem pile. Crashing a larger gem is even
> better because it breaks apart into 1-gems and sends them all at
> once. For example, if you crash a 3-gem, it splits into three 1-gems
> and all of them go to your chosen opponent's gem pile. ... The +$1 on
> the Crash Gem means you also get a little extra money to use during
> your buy phase."

So: remove one gem of value *V* from your own gem pile back to the
bank, then draw *V* separate 1-gems from the bank into a chosen
opponent's gem pile. Gem-value is conserved through the bank, not
created or destroyed by a normal crash.

### Double Crash Gem (purple, 10 in box)

Same as Crash Gem but breaks up to **two** gems from your own pile in
one play, and grants +$2 instead of +$1 (still $1 per gem actually
broken). Legal to play with only one gem in your pile — it just does
what a single Crash Gem would.

**Money from crashing is turn-restricted:** the +$1/+$2 bonus applies
only when played on your own turn (including as part of a
counter-counter-crash chain during your own crash) — *"They don't give
you money if you use them on someone else's turn."*

### Counter-crashing — a reaction, not an action

Crash Gem and Double Crash Gem double as **free reactions**, played
without spending your action, when an opponent sends gems at you:

> "Each 1-gem from your counter-crash negates a 1-gem sent by your
> opponent. Negated gems go back to bank. Any left-over 1-gems are sent
> to the player who crashed fewer gems." ... "if an opponent crashes a
> 2-gem at you and you counter-crash a 1-gem, you'd negate one of the
> two incoming 1-gems, and the other one lands in your pile... If
> instead you counter-crash a 3-gem, you'd negate both incoming 1-gems
> and send a 1-gem to your opponent."

One reaction per incoming event, but a counter-crash can itself be
counter-counter-crashed by the original attacker, chaining back and
forth. In free-for-all, if the target declines, priority to intercept
passes around the table in turn order (§2). **4-gems are immune to all
of this**: *"If you crash a 4-gem in your gem pile, no one can
counter-crash it; they can't even play a purple shield chip as a
reaction because the 4-gem is just that powerful!"* — the game's
built-in "goes over the top of the reaction layer entirely" move.

**What physically happens to crashed chips:** normal crashes cycle gem
chips bank ↔ gem piles, conserving total value — nothing is removed
from the game. Only successfully *negated* gems (from a counter-crash)
return to the bank and vanish from play at that moment. The Crash
Gem/Double Crash Gem chip itself (the action card, distinct from the
gem chips it manipulates) discards normally at Cleanup and cycles
through the deck like any other played chip.

**Crashing never touches Wounds.** It manipulates the gem pile only;
Wounds live in the separate deck cycle (§5) and are unaffected by any
Crash or Combine play.

## 8. Characters

The Third Edition base box ships exactly **ten** characters: *"Puzzle
Strike features 10 characters from the Fantasy Strike universe"* (p.1),
itemized by name in the rulebook's "Notes on Specific Chips" appendix:
**Grave, Jaina, Midori, Setsuki, Rook, DeGrey, Valerie, Geiger, Lum,
Argagarg.** **Zot, Quince, Voco, Gwen, Menelker, Onimaru, Troq,
Bal-Bas-Beta, Gloria, Persephone and Vendetta — several of which
appeared as plausible candidates going into this research — belong to
the later *Shadows* expansion or subsequent releases, not the Third
Edition base game**, and are out of scope here. Every character shares
the identical starting deck described in §4 except for exactly three
named character chips.

A second naming trap worth flagging: Sirlin Games reuses these same
character names (Grave, Setsuki, Midori, Argagarg, Geiger, DeGrey…)
across Puzzle Strike, *Yomi* (a card-based fighting game) and
*Fantasy Strike*/*Codex*, with completely different game-specific
abilities each time. Several early search hits during this research
were Yomi content (combo points, card speeds, HP) that had to be
discarded on inspection — every quote below was checked for actual
Puzzle Strike terminology (chips, gem pile, crash, Wound) before
inclusion.

Two full write-ups, chosen because they are structurally opposite —
one plays entirely on offense/tempo, one plays entirely on disruption —
plus a third with a different shape again (rule-bending its own ante),
plus two characters where only partial data was recoverable.

### Argagarg — defensive disruption

Three character chips: **Hex of Murkwood, Bubble Shield, Protective
Ward.** Official rulebook rulings (p.14–15):

- **Hex of Murkwood:** *"Each opponent chooses whether to discard two
  wounds or gain a wound. If they can't discard two wounds, they must
  gain a wound. If there are no wounds in the bank, they don't gain
  one."* — the one confirmed base-game example of a character directly
  forcing Wounds onto opponents (§5).
- **Bubble Shield** (an *ongoing* chip, §4): *"The ongoing effect of
  the bubble triggers only after your chance to counter-crash. It will
  not trigger if you counter-crash all incoming gems. If some gems are
  still incoming, the bubble's effect is not optional. The bubble
  negates only one of the 1-gems sent to you, and triggers even if the
  opponent crashes a powerful 4-gem at you."* — notably, this is the
  one documented way to blunt an otherwise-uncounterable 4-gem crash,
  because it is an ongoing passive rather than a counter-crash
  reaction.
- **Protective Ward:** *"This affects you as well as other players.
  This applies to any chip effect that says to 'combine' gems in a gem
  pile... You must discard the puzzle chips before getting the effects
  of the chip saying to combine gems."* — a standing tax/friction on
  every Combine play at the table, including the caster's own.

Community strategy guide (verbatim, "Vivafringe," [Argagarg
guide](https://forums.sirlingames.com/t/argagarg-guide/677)):

> "Argagarg is a defensive master... Strengths: Can give more wounds
> than any other character. Simple and great for beginners. Good
> natural defense against strong reds. Weaknesses: Simple gameplay
> makes it difficult to surprise expert players. Very low money.
> Relatively limited puzzle chip synergy. Gameplan: Argagarg wants to
> disrupt his opponent's plans with wounds while staying alive as long
> as possible. The longer the game drags out, the more Argagarg's
> wounds and Protective Ward hurt his opponent."

### Jaina — aggressive rushdown

Three character chips: **Playing with Fire, Burning Vigor, Unstable
Power.** Rulebook rulings (p.13):

- **Playing with Fire / Burning Vigor:** both anted from the bank
  (minor rulings only — *"The ante comes from the bank"* for each).
- **Unstable Power:** *"Just like a Double Crash, this does give you
  money if used on your turn. If someone plays Stolen Purples and you
  have Unstable Power in your hand, you don't have to discard it
  because it doesn't have a purple sphere in the banner."* — functions
  as a Double Crash Gem for gameplay purposes without being flagged as
  a "purple" chip for effects that specifically target purples.

Community strategy guide (verbatim, "Vivafringe," [Jaina
guide](https://forums.sirlingames.com/t/jaina-guide/678)):

> "Jaina didn't come to fight a calm, collected battle. Aggression is
> king queen, and defense is for wimps... Strengths: Blistering fast
> rushdown. Playing With Fire lets her use attack chips incredibly
> well. Natural resilience against wounds. Weaknesses: Vulnerable to
> good defensive characters and chips. Difficult to play. Becomes less
> powerful the more she is forced to use Unstable Power, making her a
> bad choice for the endgame. Jaina's General Gameplan: ... use attacks
> to disrupt them while you combine as much as possible. Then, use
> Unstable Power for a massive uncounterable crash to finish them off."

And on sequencing: *"Unless you are facing another aggressive
character, avoid using Unstable Power too early. You give your opponent
early pile bonus, the wounds will clog up your deck, and you let your
opponent countercrash you... your main plan should always be to crash 5
or more with Unstable Power."*

### Midori — rule-bending economy engine

Three character chips: **Dragon Form, Rigorous Training, Purge Bad
Habits.** Rulebook rulings (p.13):

- **Dragon Form:** *"The ante comes from the bank. If the bank is out
  of the gem type you need to ante, then ante a proxy gem instead.
  Dragon Form does stop opponents from reacting with Crash Gems, Double
  Crash Gems, Gems to Gemonade, Grave's Reversal, and Rook's Stone
  Wall. During Deadly Time, you still ante a 4-gem."* — an *ongoing*
  effect that changes Midori's own Ante phase (bigger, uncounterable
  antes) rather than acting on gems already in play.
- **Rigorous Training:** *"'Gained' chips go to your discard pile. This
  chip has a shield in the banner because it's a reaction. It's a
  brown shield rather than blue because it doesn't react to an
  opponent's attack."*
- **Purge Bad Habits:** *"The 'gained' chip comes from the bank."*

Community strategy guide (verbatim, "Vivafringe" & "ChumpChange,"
[Midori guide](https://forums.sirlingames.com/t/midori-strategy-guide/693)):

> "Midori is a noble dragon who focuses on training his deck to be as
> powerful as possible... Strengths: Best purple rush in the game.
> Purge Bad Habits gives you a huge amount of money for a rushdown
> character... Weaknesses: Bad at playing attack chips and weak
> defending against them... No orbs in Dragon Form is a major
> restriction... Gameplan: Midori's main plan is to tune his deck so
> that he can crash every turn in Dragon Form... Don't forget that your
> countercrashes are also uncounterable [while in Dragon Form]."

Midori is the odd one out mechanically: Argagarg and Jaina both play
entirely within the ordinary crash/combine/buy vocabulary; Dragon Form
*changes what the Ante phase itself does* for as long as it's active —
closer to a mode switch than an ordinary action.

### Grave and Setsuki — partial data only

Official rulings only; no reliable strategy-guide text without risking
Yomi cross-contamination (§ above).

- **Grave** — Reversal, Martial Mastery, Versatile Style. *Reversal:*
  *"This only works when gems are 'sent' to you. Crashing or
  counter-crashing gems in a gem pile 'sends' gems but other operations
  like Sneak Attack do not."* *Martial Mastery:* *"It can't trash
  itself or other character chips."* Versatile Style: no substantive
  ruling recovered. **[a low-confidence, unverifiable BGG thread
  paraphrase suggested Reversal functions "as a crash gem to counter
  gems sent to you" — consistent with the ruling above but not
  independently confirmed, since the thread itself returned a 403]**
- **Setsuki** — Speed of the Fox, Bag of Tricks, Double-take. The
  rulebook's own rulings for the first two are throwaway ("It's pretty
  self-explanatory!" / "Have fun.") and their actual printed text
  **could not be found** from any reliable source in the time
  available. *Double-take:* *"If you gain extra actions (+arrows) with
  this chip, you can't actually use them because your action phase
  ends before you can. If you Double-take an attack, each instance
  requires a separate reaction."* — implies a reaction chip that
  duplicates or responds to an opponent's action; full base text
  **unverified**.

## 9. Panic Time — the game's own clock

As the shared bank's individual chip stacks run dry (a proxy for "this
game has gone long"), the forced per-turn Ante escalates, permanently
and in one direction only, at thresholds scaled to player count
(roughly *X*/*X*+1/*X*+2 simultaneously-empty stacks for *X* players):
first to 2-gems ("Panic Time"), then 3-gems ("Danger Time"), then
4-gems ("Deadly Time"). (p.8) This mechanically guarantees escalation
toward someone crossing 10 rather than a game that can stall forever —
functionally the same job Mage Knight's dummy-player round clock does,
by a completely different mechanism (bank depletion instead of a
countdown deck).

## 10. Win condition and game length, summary

Already stated precisely in §6; restated as a single summary. A player
loses the instant they end their own turn with gem-pile value ≥10.
2-player: immediate win/loss, game over. 3–4p free-for-all: the whole
game ends the instant *anyone* crosses the threshold, and the winner is
whoever currently holds the *lowest* total across all players — not a
last-survivor format by default. 2v2: a shared team pile. ~20 minutes
per two independent secondary sources; the theoretical floor, per
Sirlin's own design note, is 10 turns if nobody ever plays a Crash Gem
at all, since every turn antes exactly 1 toward that total absent any
crashing.

## 11. Puzzle Strike 2 — a different game, confirmed by the designer

Flagged because the objective's framing ("2nd edition or 'remake'") could
plausibly have meant this. It is not an edition of the base game covered
above — it is a full redesign, per Sirlin's own words on
[sirlin.net](https://www.sirlin.net/puzzle-strike-2) / the
[Sirlin Games PS2 page](https://www.sirlingames.com/ps2): *"enough
significant changes to the gameplay to make it a wholly different
experience"* — he calls them *"really just two different games."*
Confirmed differences: gems are colored rather than purely numeric;
crashing removes your topmost pile gem **plus adjacent same-color gems
in a run** and sends that run's total value to opponents (this *is*
close to the "N chips of matching value crash together" mechanic
originally guessed for this document — it belongs to PS2, not the base
game); a new "Scepter of Power" mechanic adds King-of-Tokyo-style
target-the-leader dynamics; cards replace chips in places; billed 1–4
players (including solo) at 20–30 minutes. PS2 keeps the same core
"pile ≥10 at turn-end = lose" loss condition and the ten-character
framing, per a [GeekDad preview](https://geekdad.com/2022/02/kickstarter-tabletop-alert-puzzle-strike-2/).
Treated here as a sibling product, not a source for "the stable base
rules" — everything in §1–10 above is Third Edition only.

Separately, an **actual 2nd→3rd Edition rebalance** (same underlying
game as this document, not PS2) is referenced by a forum thread,
[Differences between 2nd edition and latest](https://forums.sirlingames.com/t/differences-between-2nd-edition-and-latest/3429)
— fetched only as a paraphrased summary, not the raw thread text, so
treated as **[medium-confidence]**: the Combine chip's cost changed to
-$1 (described as the change that prompted several other rebalances),
several Puzzle Chips were reworded, and 3rd Edition widened Crash Gem
targeting to any opponent (implying earlier editions restricted it to
one's left-hand neighbor, per the independent Club Fantasci review).
"Nearly all characters received changes except Setsuki" between
editions, per the same thread. None of this affects §1–10, which are
sourced from the 3rd Edition rulebook directly.
