# Puzzle Strike — the chips, transcribed

Read off the photographs in this folder, which are the owner's own set, and
cross-checked against `list of all cards.txt` — the owner's own inventory,
which is what settles every name here. Where a photo of a physical chip
disagrees with a printed sheet the photo wins; where a chip is obscured or
absent it is listed under *Missing* rather than guessed at.

`rules.md` is the rulebook; this file is the components.

**Two editions are in play and it matters.** The physical chips photographed
(`most cards scan.jpg`, `more cards.jpg`, the `Screenshot_*.png` set,
`even more cards.webp`, `argarga and fox.webp`) are **third edition**. The
sheet `all base characters v1.webp` is **version 4.7, © 2010** — an earlier
printing, and several chips were reworded after it. Third-edition text is used
wherever a physical chip was photographed; the v1 sheet fills the gaps and every
entry it is the only source for is marked **[2nd ed]**. §*Edition differences*
below lists every place the two disagree.

`Screenshot_20260823_101124.png` photographs a second and third edition chip
side by side and is what dates the costs: One of Each went 6 → **5**, One-Two
Punch 5 → **4**, Iron Defense 5 → **4**. Every cost here is the third-edition
one.

## The symbol vocabulary

Every chip says what it gives in icons rather than words, and four icons cover
almost everything:

| Icon | Means |
|---|---|
| black arrow | +1 action this turn |
| coloured arrow (yellow / red / blue / purple / brown) | +1 action, restricted to a chip whose banner is that colour |
| piggy bank | +1 buy this turn |
| dark filled circle | +1 chip drawn |
| green `$N` | +N gem power (money) this turn |
| green square with a number | a gem of that value |
| black puzzle piece | a Puzzle chip |
| coloured dot | a chip with that banner colour |

Sirlin's own change document names the banner shapes, which the photographs
only show: a **fist** is an attack (red), a **shield** is a reaction (blue, or
purple for the crash family), and an **orb** is an ordinary play. So "purple
orb" means a Crash Gem or Combine played on your turn and "purple shield" means
one played as a counter-crash — two different things a chip may forbid, and
Knockdown forbids only the second.

`One of Each` is the proof: cost 5, one of each of the four — an arrow, a buy,
`$1`, and a chip. A banner's colour is also its type: red is an attack, blue a
reaction, purple the crash family, brown/tan plain.

## Two rules the change document settles

**Panic Time is a ratchet.** X is the number of players; X empty bank stacks
antes 2-gems, X+1 antes 3-gems, X+2 antes 4-gems — and *"empty stacks refilling
does not revert the ante."* Nothing in the built game refills a stack, so
counting empties is the same number either way there; a game that adds Into
Oblivion would need the high-water mark kept rather than recomputed.

**Three and four players** is a bigger change than the player count: you may
crash to anyone, everybody gets a chance to react in turn order starting with
the target, other people's crashes give you no money, the moment somebody is
eliminated the *lowest* pile wins outright, and gems sent to a player already at
ten or more **float** until their pile drops to nine or is destroyed at end of
turn. rules.md §6 has the same rules from the rulebook.
